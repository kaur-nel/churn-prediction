## Problem

A telecom company loses customers (churn). Retention offers cost money, and only help customers who would have left. The question: which customers should receive an offer, and does a targeted campaign beat the alternatives?

## Data

- IBM Telco Customer Churn sample dataset (Kaggle): 7,043 customers, split into 4,225 train, 1,409 validation and 1,409 test (stratified).
- Test split churn rate: 26.5% (374 churners).
- Provenance, collection method and time period are not verified; treat the dataset as a public example.

## Business impact (placeholder assumptions)

Assumed: customer lifetime value 1,000, offer cost 50, offer success rate 30%. None of these is company data.

- An offer pays for itself when the churn probability is above 0.167 (offer cost / (success rate x lifetime value)).
- The threshold 0.171 was chosen on the validation split by maximising net benefit.
- On validation, net benefit at the break-even probability was 43,825 per 1,000 customers, against 44,038 at the chosen threshold.
- Validation expected 44,038; the test result is 45,493, which is inside the test confidence interval.
- The lower end of the test interval (38,963) is above the point estimate for offering everyone (29,631). This is an unpaired comparison, not a formal test.

## Recommendation

This is the analyst's recommendation, and it depends on the placeholder assumptions above.

1. **Run a controlled pilot,** not a full rollout. Flag customers with a score of at least 0.171 (about 51.7% of customers) and keep a randomly chosen group of flagged customers untreated.
2. **Measure the real offer success rate.** The assumed 30% drives every money figure; replace it with the measured value and re-run the business step.
3. **Review the offer design for `SeniorCitizen` groups** before launch: gaps in false-positive rate, share flagged exceed 0.10.
4. **Monitor drift** with `python -m src.drift` on each new batch; investigate any feature at or above PSI 0.25 and consider retraining.
5. **Do not change the model or threshold because of the test results.** Any improvement should be developed on validation data and then evaluated on fresh data.

## Limitations and next steps

- All money figures rest on assumptions. A churn score does not say who will respond to an offer.
- One dataset of unknown provenance with no time information; the test split holds 374 churners, so the confidence intervals are wide.
- The threshold was chosen on a single validation split. Net benefit is usually flat near the optimum, and the sensitivity table shows how the best threshold moves with the assumptions.
- Next steps: measure the offer effect with a randomized test, add behavioural or time-based features if available, consider uplift modelling (who responds to an offer), and define a retraining schedule.
