## Intended use

- Rank existing telecom customers by estimated risk of leaving, to help a retention team decide who receives a retention offer.
- Use with human oversight and a measured campaign (see `docs/report.md`), not as an automatic decision.

## Out-of-scope use

- Decisions about a person's access to service, pricing or credit.
- Other companies, countries or time periods without re-validation: the model learned from one public example dataset.
- Explaining *why* customers leave. The SHAP explanations describe what the model does, not causes.
- Using the money figures as forecasts: they rest on placeholder assumptions (see below).

## Data

- **Source:** the IBM Telco Customer Churn sample dataset (as distributed on Kaggle).
- **Provenance not verified:** we do not know how it was collected, which period it covers, or whether it describes real customers. Treat it as a public example dataset.
- **Test split:** 1,409 customers, 374 churners (26.5%). Splits are stratified by churn.
- **Cleaning (see `src/data.py`):** spaces trimmed; a blank `TotalCharges` for a customer with tenure 0 set to 0 (an assumption); "No internet service" and "No phone service" merged into "No"; exact duplicate rows dropped; values outside the expected ranges or categories rejected.

## Features

- **Numeric:** `tenure`, `MonthlyCharges`, `charges_ratio`, `addon_count`
- **Yes/no flags:** `SeniorCitizen`, `is_month_to_month`, `is_electronic_check`, `is_fiber_optic`, `month_to_month_new`
- **Categories (one-hot encoded):** `Partner`, `Dependents`, `PhoneService`, `MultipleLines`, `InternetService`, `OnlineSecurity`, `OnlineBackup`, `DeviceProtection`, `TechSupport`, `StreamingTV`, `StreamingMovies`, `Contract`, `PaperlessBilling`, `PaymentMethod`, `tenure_bucket`
- **Left out on purpose:** `gender` (no signal in the exploratory analysis and a protected attribute; kept in the data only for the fairness check), the customer ID and the target.
- **Dropped for this model only:** `TotalCharges` (nearly redundant with tenure and monthly charges; see the variance-inflation check).
- `SeniorCitizen` **is** a model feature.
- Engineered features are defined in `src/features.py`; none is computed from the target.

## Training and selection procedure

1. Stratified 60%/20%/20% split before any fitting; imputing, scaling and encoding are fitted on training data only.
2. Imbalance strategies (no imbalance handling, class weights, SMOTE) and four model families compared with 5-fold cross-validation on the training split.
3. The two best models were tuned with Optuna (30 trials each) and checked on the validation split.
4. Calibration methods were compared on the validation split, and the one with the best Brier score was kept only if it beat the uncalibrated model by the configured margin.
5. The threshold was chosen on the validation split by maximising expected net benefit.
6. The test split was then scored **once** with the frozen model and threshold.

## Limitations

- The money figures depend on three assumed numbers. The offer success rate in particular must be measured with a controlled test.
- A churn score says who is likely to leave, not who will respond to an offer.
- One dataset of unknown provenance; no information about when customers left. The test split holds 374 churners, so scores carry the uncertainty shown by the confidence intervals.
- The threshold was chosen on a single validation split; the exact optimum is uncertain.
- SHAP explains the model, not the world; overlapping features share credit, and small groups give noisy effects.
- Fairness was checked for the listed attributes on one test split; any flagged gap above should be reviewed before deployment.
